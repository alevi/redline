# Proposal for the First Fully Unnecessary Polar Expedition

## Executive Summary

This document proposes a polar expedition to a location that has already been visited, thoroughly documented, and declared uninteresting by three separate scientific bodies. We intend to go anyway.

The expedition has no scientific objectives. It has a *vibe*.

## The Destination

We will travel to a specific, unnamed ice shelf in the high Arctic that appears on maps only as a series of dashes and the notation "do not linger." The notation's origin is unclear. We will linger.

## Team Composition

The expedition will consist of seven members, selected not for their survival skills but for their strong opinions about equipment and their willingness to document disagreements at length.

Key roles:

- **The Expedition Leader** — responsible for morale, which they will approach with the energy of someone who has read too many biographies of Apsley Cherry-Garrard
- **The Chronicler** — a writer whose only previous cold-weather experience was a delayed flight through Minneapolis
- **The Provisions Specialist** — has firm views on which hard cheeses travel well and will share them unprompted
- **Four Generalists** — capable people who will, over time, develop highly specific and conflicting opinions about the correct way to pitch a tent
- **The Skeptic** — included deliberately, to keep everyone honest

## Equipment Philosophy

The expedition will bring everything that is considered essential and nothing that is considered sensible. This includes:

- A portable espresso machine (non-negotiable)
- A compact hand grinder (e.g., Timemore C2 or 1Zpresso JX)
- 15 kinds of rope (the Provisions Specialist insisted)
- A set of watercolor paints, because the light at those latitudes is said to be extraordinary
- One pair of snowshoes more than necessary, as a psychological buffer
- A hand-cranked shortwave radio that nobody has tested but everyone agrees looks correct

No GPS. The Chronicler argued that GPS removes narrative tension. The team voted 5–2 in favor of this position. The two dissenters have been noted.

## The Journey

Travel will proceed in three phases:

**Phase One: Getting There**
This phase involves a commercial flight to the nearest reasonable airport, followed by a charter to a significantly less reasonable one, followed by a snowmobile transfer that will take twice as long as quoted and be described by the driver, Håkon, as "fine, mostly."

**Phase Two: The Ice**
The team will establish a base camp at coordinates to be determined on arrival, because planning too specifically feels like tempting fate. Each morning will begin with espresso and a brief group discussion of whether the previous day's decisions were correct. They were not. This will be documented.

**Phase Three: Coming Back**
Less glamorous than Phase Two. Nobody writes about Phase Three. We will write about Phase Three.

## Success Criteria

The expedition will be considered successful if:

1. Everyone returns
2. The Chronicler produces at least 40,000 words
3. At least one genuine disagreement about rope occurs
4. The espresso machine survives
5. Someone sees something they cannot explain and chooses not to try
6. All collected specimens and data reach the archive intact
7. No permanent environmental damage is caused at any site visited
8. A full written account is completed and filed within 30 days of return

## Why This Expedition

Because the places that have already been mapped still exist. Because the point of going somewhere difficult is not always to discover something new — sometimes it is to find out what you are like when the options narrow. Because fourteen kinds of rope is, objectively, too many, and someone should find out why.

The ice doesn't care if we come. That's the point.
